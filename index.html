What breaks when a mosquito model leaves home

A recursive research loop on global mosquito habitat mapping. Climate, landscape, human footprint and AlphaEarth satellite embeddings are fused and tested under nine ways of holding out the world. The loop found where transfer fails: not the fusion layer, and not the global-to-local hierarchy, but which species have arrived in each region.

The loop

Each experiment is chosen by the last one's failure

The task is unchanged from HiCo-Fuse: for each 0.05° cell with an iNaturalist mosquito report (7,004 cells, 2022–2025), predict the share of reports that are Ae. albopictus, Ae. aegypti, Culex, Anopheles or other Culicidae. Conditioning on a report cancels observer effort that is equal across species. Metrics are mean per-class AUC and record-weighted log-loss. Validation and diagnosis (highlighted) carried most of the information this round.

Iteration 1 · validation and baselines

Random-split skill is mostly interpolation

Nine baselines on the same 105-feature fused representation, under nine holdout designs. The random forest ranks first on every design. Random splits overstate transfer skill by 0.10 AUC for the best model and 0.26 for pure spatial interpolation, which falls to chance (0.52) when a whole region is held out.

Iteration 2 · learned weighting

Learned weights fit the training geography

Five experts (Climate, Landscape, Human, AlphaEarth MLPs and a geographic interpolator) combined by a log-linear pool. Seven weighting rules. Every learned rule was fitted on inner out-of-fold predictions of the training set only, so the gap between training-side gain and held-out gain measures how much the weighting itself overfits.

Training-side vs held-out gain over equal weights (leave region out)

Log-loss gain relative to equal weighting; right of zero is better. Only local reliability weighting keeps a held-out gain.

The support-aware gate trusts interpolation more far from data

Mean gate weight on the geographic expert by distance to training data. The inner folds contained almost no far-from-data cells, so the gate extrapolated in its own input space. Global stacking gave the geographic expert 0.42 of the weight for the same reason.

Iterations 4–5 · two redesigns

Structure beats flexibility, but not the forest

Iteration 4 fixed the two causes found in iteration 2. Learned weights are now trained on inner predictions that include leave-a-training-region-out folds (regime-matched). A monotone support-decay pool replaces the free gate. The geographic expert's weight decays as exp(−d/ρ). Covariate experts fade toward the class prior as environmental novelty grows. There are about 15 parameters in total.

Paired over 20 splits: support decay beats the free support gate in 17/20 splits on AUC (p = 0.003) and 17/20 on log-loss. It ties equal pooling (10/20, p = 1.00) and beats the random forest in only 8/20. Regime matching changes what is learned: median correlation range ρ is 1,822 km when trained on block folds and 714 km when trained on mixed folds. It does not change accuracy significantly (12/20, p = 0.50).

Iteration 5 tested the iteration-1 diagnosis directly. It added an exogenous species-pool signal: log record density of the Kraemer literature compendium (1960–2014, not iNaturalist) within 100 km and 500 km for the two Aedes species. The prediction was a larger gain under transfer than in-distribution.

Iteration 6 · coherence versus accuracy

Coherence is free; HiCo's learners are the cost

The random forest beats HiCo on every holdout, but HiCo was built for something accuracy does not measure: a city-level map that never contradicts the country-level map. This iteration asks whether that guarantee is what costs accuracy. Each arm is scored on 0.05° cells and on 0.5° parents, and every arm except the first satisfies HiCo's exact rule that a parent's intensity equals the average of its children's.

Reconciliation costs nothing measurable. Re-normalising the forest under HiCo's rule is within noise of the plain forest (9/20 AUC wins, mean -0.004, p = 0.82; log-loss +0.006). Mapping the two scales separately instead makes them disagree: mean composition distance 0.06, and the dominant class flips in 4–7% of parents. Original HiCo loses in 18/20 splits (p < 0.001). Giving HiCo the forest as its local term helps (wins 6/20) and is the best arm under 10° blocks, but its jointly learned macro network falls behind under region and zone holdouts, the same failure as the learned weights in iteration 2.

One more result changes what the hierarchy is for. A coarse map built as the plain average of the forest's fine map is as accurate as a separately trained coarse forest (12/20, p = 0.50). When the coarse level has no data of its own, the hierarchy adds nothing beyond aggregation. It earns its place only when the coarse term is fed evidence the fine model cannot see, such as country checklists or surveillance by administrative unit. That is the macro term of the Pool–Niche–Support proposal.

Synthesis

Limitations that persist across every method tried

  1. Regional class-prior shift dominates transfer error. The true regional class mix closes almost all of the forest's held-out log-loss gap. No fusion, weighting or invariance scheme changes this.
  2. Weights learned where data are dense do not transfer. Fusion weights must be learned on inner predictions whose distance and novelty profile matches deployment, and constrained to behave monotonically with support.
  3. AlphaEarth is strongest in-region and weakest out of it. Its residual is a regional fingerprint; it should dominate near data and fade under novelty.
  4. Neural extrapolation is unstable. The MLP's Europe hold-out AUC moved from 0.53 to 0.71 with only the seed changed. Tree ensembles extrapolate conservatively and are the bar to beat.
  5. Coherence is a constraint, not a learner. HiCo's normalisation applied to the forest keeps its accuracy and removes every cross-scale contradiction; HiCo's own neural terms are what cost accuracy (iteration 6).
  6. Data deserts set the ceiling. Africa has 75 report cells and Latin America is weakest for every model; no architecture fixes absent evidence.

Proposed next architecture: Pool–Niche–Support (PNS)

log λs(x) = as(R(x)) + fs(x) + k(d(x)) · rs(x), with HiCo normalisation over each 0.5° parent
QuestionAnswer for PNS
Limitation addressedLimitation 1 (regional prior shift) and 2 (support-regime mismatch); iteration 1 Table 4, iteration 2, iteration 4.
What is newA regional species-pool latent as, partially pooled across regions and informed by exogenous checklists, kept separate from the niche term fs. Plus a spatial residual whose influence decays with a learned correlation range, all inside the exact-coherence hierarchy.
Why it fits habitat mappingRange = niche × dispersal history. Composition models conflate the two; PNS gives each its own term and its own evidence source.
Assumption encodedNiche relations are shared across regions; arrival history is regional; spatial residual dependence is local (Tobler) and monotone in distance.
How it differsUnlike stacking or gating, weights cannot grow with distance. Unlike coordinate covariates, the regional term has an external prior instead of being extrapolated. Unlike plain HiCo, the macro term is not learned from the same reports.
AblationsDrop as; replace its exogenous prior with a uniform one; drop the decay (free gate); drop rs; random vs regime-matched inner folds.
Where it should winLeave region out and climate zone out, on log-loss first. It should tie the random forest under 10° blocks.
What would falsify itNo log-loss gain over RF on held-out regions in ≥4 of 6 regions, or the gain vanishes when the exogenous prior is replaced by a uniform one (meaning the pool term is not doing the work).

Independent research prototype · not an official NASA product

Data. Mosquito reports: iNaturalist research-grade Culicidae observations 2022–2025 (via GBIF), aggregated to 0.05° cells. Covariates: WorldClim-style bioclimatic variables, landscape and human-footprint layers, Google AlphaEarth Foundations 2024 embeddings. External records: Kraemer et al. (2015) Aedes compendium. No observation coordinates are published here.

Code and benchmarks. github.com/missa7481/NASA_Global_Habitat_Mapping: model development (HiCo-Fuse, OA-DSDM, research loop) and all benchmark tables.

Method. Every number on this page is generated from experiment output files. Holdout designs: random, 5°/10°/20° spatial blocks, leave one region out, environmental cluster, climate zone, temporal and space-time.
